# Your day and your week

**Home** is where a day starts in Margince. It has two views, **Morning** and
**Weekly**, and two scopes, **Mine** and **Team**. The team scope appears only
if you can see records past your own.

The Worklist is the ranked to-do list in Margince: one list of everything
waiting on you, in the order Margince ranks them. That is buyers to answer,
tasks due, promises made, deals at risk and items to check. It lives on **Home**.
Press **Show Worklist** to open it as a panel over the page; **Hide Worklist** closes it.
It has no sidebar row of its own.

## Daily tasks

### How do I open my worklist?
To open your Worklist in Margince, go to **Home** in the sidebar and press **Show Worklist**. The Worklist opens as a panel headed **Worklist**, with the day's count on the button.
1. Click **Home** in the sidebar.
2. Press **Show Worklist**. Press **Hide Worklist** to close it.
3. A team lead can switch **Whose work** between **Mine**, **Unassigned**, **Team** and **All**.

There is no separate Worklist page, Today page or Inbox page. Also called: to-do list, my tasks, work queue, inbox, today list.

### How do I snooze something on my worklist?
To snooze a Worklist item in Margince, press **Snooze** on its row. It comes back tomorrow ("Back on your list tomorrow.").
1. Open **Home** → **Show Worklist**.
2. On the row, press **Snooze** for one day. Or open the arrow beside it (**Snooze duration**) for more choices.
3. Choose **Snooze for 3 days**, **Snooze for 7 days** or **Until they reply**.
4. The note that follows offers **Undo**.

A snooze changes your list only. A reply that came in before you snoozed does not end it. Also called: remind me later, postpone, hide for now.

### How do I pin an item to the top of my worklist?
To pin a Worklist item in Margince, press **Pin** on its row. It moves to the top of your Worklist.
1. Open **Home** → **Show Worklist**.
2. Press **Pin** on the row. Press **Unpin** to put it back in its ranked place.

A pin changes the order of your own list only, and changes nothing for colleagues; how urgent it is stays the same. You hold at most 50 pins: a newer one takes the place of your oldest. If it fails: "Item was not pinned. Retry." Also called: star, flag, keep on top.

### How do I remove something from my worklist?
To take an item off your Worklist in Margince, open **Remove from list** on its row and choose **Not mine** or **Not a customer**.
1. Open **Home** → **Show Worklist** and find the row.
2. Open **Remove from list**.
3. Choose **Not mine** ("Removed from your list. The owner still sees it.") or **Not a customer** ("Removed from every list.").
4. Press **Undo** in the note if you picked wrong.

**Not a customer** removes the whole thread for everyone, and does not run out. Also called: dismiss, delete from worklist, hide, not relevant.

### How do I undo a snooze or removal on my worklist?
To undo a Worklist snooze or removal in Margince, press **Undo** in the note that appears right after you picked it.

The undo reaches as far as the choice did. Undoing your own snooze never brings back a thread a colleague marked **Not a customer** for the whole company. If the undo fails, the item stays off your list ("Undo failed. The message is still off your list."). Snoozed items also come back on their own when the snooze ends. Also called: restore, bring back, unsnooze.

### How do I see all my tasks?
To see all your open tasks in Margince, open **Home**, press **Show Worklist**, and choose the **Tasks** button under **Work type**.
Tasks are in groups by when they are due. Late ones and today's come first, then **Due tomorrow**, **Due this week** and **Later**. There is no separate "Due today" heading.

A task more than 30 days late leaves the Worklist and its counts. A pin keeps it there while the pin is one of your 50 newest. The task stays open, keeps its due date, and still shows on its record.
A company also has a **Tasks** tab with its own tasks; a contact has no Tasks tab.
Also called: my to-dos, tasks due today, open tasks, task list.

### How do I complete a task?
To complete a task in Margince, press **Done** on its Worklist row, or on the task in the record's list of next steps.
1. Open **Home** → **Show Worklist**, or open the contact, company or deal the task is on.
2. Press **Done**. The task is marked "Task completed".
3. Not ready yet? Press **Snooze 1 day** to move its due date a day later. Or open the task and pick a day under **Move to**.

**Snooze 1 day** adds a day to the task's own due date, so a task with no date does not show it. If it fails: "Task was not completed. Retry." Also called: tick off, mark done, close a task.

### How do I plan my week?
To plan your week in Margince, open **Home**, switch the view to **Weekly** (scope **Mine**) and add items under **This week’s commitments**.
1. Click **Home**, then **Weekly**.
2. Under **This week’s commitments**, press **Add commitment** and fill in **Commitment**.
3. If you like, add a **Due date** and a linked deal, lead, contact, company or project, then press **Add**.
4. Tick commitments to mark them, then press **Save {count} changes**.
5. Fill in **Constraints this week**: **Risks** and **Available capacity**.

Also called: weekly plan, weekly goals, set commitments.

### How do I see my weekly review?
To read your weekly review in Margince, open **Home** and switch the view to **Weekly**. The review of your last closed week sits above your plan.

Margince writes the review on the Monday after the week, and then it is **Frozen**; you cannot make it again. Before your first full week it says "No weekly review yet. The first one is created on the Monday after your first full week." A team lead switches the scope to **Team** for the team's week. Also called: weekly report, week in review, weekly summary.

## The Worklist

A lead record does not come into the Worklist because someone created it,
imported it or set its owner. A planned task linked to a lead, or a request that came in, can
come in through the list of tasks or of waiting messages. Choose prospects in
**Leads**, then plan when to reach out.

The Worklist is one ranked list. Margince sets the order, and the screen never
changes it.

### The three bands

Worklist items are in bands, shown in this order:

| Band | When it is clear it says |
|---|---|
| **Now** | "No urgent items. Remaining work is below." |
| **Keep momentum** | "No agreed work is drifting." |
| **Prospecting** | "No prospecting work waiting." |

Work to check is one more kind. It does not show a band in the list; it has its
own panel under it, headed **To review**.

A band you have cleared still shows its heading and says so, but only when the
whole day is loaded.

Inside a band, work due tomorrow, this week or later has its own heading. Late
work and today's work sit under the band heading, because that is what the band
already means.

A row that does not fit moves down, never up. When one band fills, the rows that
do not fit drop to Keep momentum. They never go before work that was already
urgent.

### Why a row is where it is
Each Worklist row explains itself three ways, in sentences, not under headings:

- **The facts that put it there**: a buyer wrote last, a promise is due, a
  meeting is close, nobody has replied in so many days.
- **Why it ranks above the row under it**, written out: "Ranked above the next
  item because you pinned it."
- **What happens if you leave it**: "If ignored, the buyer keeps waiting."

The rank comes from a fixed ladder of checks, not from one number. A pin comes
first, then the band, then whether the row did not fit its band. Next comes what
**kind** of work it is. It may be waiting on someone, a promise, a risk to real
money, something agreed, something that holds up a colleague, or daily work.
Then comes its due date, then the money it may bring, and so on down.

Each step decides before the next one is looked at. Nothing adds up into a
single number you cannot take apart.

### Pinning

A pin moves a Worklist row to the top of your Worklist and keeps it there. It
is yours alone: pinning changes the order of what you can already read, and
changes nothing for anyone else. How urgent it is stays the same. The audit log
still records the pin.

**A pin never runs out.** It lasts longer than the row it names, and nothing
takes one off for you. The one exception: only your 50 newest pins count, so
pinning one more makes your oldest stop counting.

### Taking something off the list

Taking an item off the Worklist uses three choices, and they reach different
numbers of users:

| Choice | What it does | Who it touches |
|---|---|---|
| **Snooze** | Comes back tomorrow, in 3 days, in 7, or when they reply | **Your list only** |
| **Not mine** | "Removed from your list. The owner still sees it." | **Your list only** |
| **Not a customer** | "Removed from every list." | **Everybody** |

"Not a customer" is judged on the **thread**, not the message, so the next reply
in it comes in already judged.

Each says what it did ("Back on your list tomorrow.", "Back on your list when
they reply.") and each offers **Undo**. The undo reaches as far as the choice
did. Clearing your own snooze never lets back in a thread a colleague ruled out
for the whole company.

A reply that came in *before* you snoozed does not end the snooze.

### "List changed"

The Worklist holds still while you work through it. Something may come in, or
someone may deal with something while you work. Then a notice headed **List
changed** says which: "{arrived} new since you opened the list. Refresh to add
them."

The page is still right, only not complete. The Refresh button appears only when
something came in.

### How complete is this?
The Worklist footer says how complete the list is, in one of two ways:

- **"{shown} of {considered} shown"**: a plain share.
- **"{shown} shown · {sources} sources have more"**: a source stopped at its
  reading limit.

The second shows no share, because nobody knows the total.

If Margince could not read a source at all, a box headed
**Worklist incomplete** names it above the list. The empty list then reads
**"No items in the sources that loaded."** instead of "Nothing is waiting on
you."

### Handled for you

**Handled for you**, on the Worklist, lists what Margince did for you in the
last 24 hours: the action, the record, when, and an **Undo**.

When there is nothing: "No actions taken on your behalf today."

**Undo** puts the record back the way it was before the change. It is offered on
deals; other kinds of entry show the row without one. An entry already undone
says **"Already undone"** instead of leaving the list, so you can see that it
was.

## For a team lead

A team lead in Margince, or anyone who can see records past their own, sees more
on Home and in the Worklist.

On **Home → Team** you get the team board and the coaching suggestions. The rest
lives in the Worklist panel, which has its own **Whose work** switch (**Mine**,
**Unassigned**, **Team**, **All**). **Hidden from the Worklist** appears only at
**All**.

**Team exceptions** is team work that has gone past a line. A first reply is
late, money is at risk, nobody has taken it, or the same thing keeps failing.
Each row names **what it was judged against**, so you can see the bar instead of
guessing it. You can take a row on from here.

**Team work needing attention** has a row per teammate: waiting on a reply, deals
at risk, past due, promises due. Unassigned work gets its own row.

**Coaching suggestions** has at most one suggestion per teammate: the most
urgent kind, not the one with the most work.

**Hidden from the Worklist** lists the reasons work is held back, each with what
it costs you:
- **Too old for the Worklist**: no one decided this; the sender wrote months
  back and nobody answered
- **Not linked to a record**
- **From your company’s domains**
- **Marked not sales work**
- **Set aside by you**

When nothing is held back, it says: "Nothing is hidden. Every waiting customer
reaches a Worklist."

You can also add a note to a teammate's Worklist with **Add note** ("A short
note that appears in their Worklist.").

## The weekly review

The weekly review in Margince is on **Home → Weekly**. Margince writes it once,
on the Monday after the week it covers, and marks it **Frozen**. You cannot make
it again: the counts are what they were.

If there is none yet: "No weekly review yet. The first one is created on the
Monday after your first full week."

Above everything sits this rule:

> **Recorded CRM work for this closed week. Missing records do not establish
> inactivity.**

### What it counts
The weekly review counts won, lost, stage changes, and tasks completed and
carried over. It counts plan commitments kept, recorded replies to leads, and
meetings with a linked next step. Each shows next to the week before. Where
there is no week before, the review leaves that out, instead of showing a change
from nothing.

Behind a button sit more parts: a scorecard, the forecast and how the week moved
it, and the observations.

### The scorecard

**Leads and meetings**: leads moved on, recorded replies (with how many missed
the target), meetings held (with booked and missed meetings under them).

**Deals** shows stage moves on and back, and how many days a deal most often stays
in a stage. It shows how many open deals have a next step, and how many have more than
one contact. It shows how many have a close date that is not a guess. And it shows forecast moves up
against moves down.

Two rows appear only when a count is not complete:

- **Meetings without history**: "Before history began · counts are minimums".
- **Deals not rebuilt**: "Affected by an erasure · counts are minimums".

Margince cannot count deals touched by a deletion for the week. It lists them on
their own.

### Observations to review

**Observations to review** holds at most four observations. Each names the deals
it was read from, and each has a tag: **Positive outcome**, **Unsuccessful
outcome**, **Pattern**, or **Experiment**.

Each carries a warning: the observations show links in recorded
work, and do not show what made the outcome happen.

Two empty states mean different things. **"This week has not been analyzed
yet."** means the reading has not run. **"Not enough recorded evidence for
useful observations."** means there were fewer than three records to name, so no
model was asked.

An installation with no AI model set up still gets the whole review. It only
never gets the observations or the written summary, and says so.

## Planning a week

**This week’s commitments**, on **Home → Weekly**, is your plan for the week:
what you said you will do, and what you need to do it.

Each commitment has a name, and can have a linked record and a due date. Ticking
one marks it but does not save it. Nothing is written until you press **Save
{count} changes**. If some fail, the ones that failed stay ticked, with a count.

### The four states

**Open**, **Done**, **Dropped** and **Missed**.

You set the first three. You cannot mark a commitment missed: Margince marks open
commitments missed when the week closes.

**Dropped** is your decision, and it counts as not kept and not owed.

### What the week is up against
**Constraints this week** holds two fields for your own text. **Risks** asks
what you expect could go wrong, in your own words. **Available capacity** asks
for anything the calendar does not show, such as time off.

Each has three states: **Not written yet**, **"Nothing to name"**, or the text.
A question nobody answered and an answer of "nothing" are different facts.

If the week already looks full, it says so under **Week already full**. It
refuses nothing: "{committed} items are already booked and {commitments}
commitments are planned. Something will not fit."

### Asking your lead for help

To ask your lead for help, press **Ask for help** on a commitment, answer
**"What do you need from your lead?"** and press **Send**. While it is open and
nothing has come back, the row reads "Help requested · awaiting a response". The
answer comes on that same row, with the name of whoever wrote it.

**Nothing in the product tells your lead.**
They find the request when they open your plan, or on the next Monday, where it
is the first thing on their list for you. If you need an answer before
that, ask them yourself as well.

(An installation that has connected its own automation to the events Margince
sends can let them know about the request; nothing built in does that.)

A lead may answer a commitment and nothing else. A lead cannot close it,
change its words or drop it.

## The team's week

A team's week in Margince is on **Home → Weekly** with the scope set to
**Team**. A lead looking at a team sees the same week from the other side. It
shows reply rates, meetings with a recorded next step, commitments kept, won and
lost, and how many members were counted.

**Monday agenda** has one line per teammate, the most urgent first. Each line
gives the reason it is there:
- **Help requested**: they asked for help
- **Response targets missed**
- **Plan commitments missed**
- **Deal recovery**: deals at risk
- **Strong week**: worth copying
- **Follow-up evidence missing**: meetings with no next step
- **No priority identified**: a quiet week

**Copy agenda** copies it in one press.

The page carries two notes about missing data:

- Where member data is missing, it says for how many team members, and how
  many members the numbers cover. It is never hidden behind a button.
- Where nobody was measured, it says there is no member data for this week, so
  nothing is measured. The page does not show a row of 0s.

A team's week is for the team's lead, and for a role that looks after every team
(Admin and Management, as they come). Reaching every record is not enough. A
read-only seat sees the whole company and its team's live work on Morning, and
is still not offered the team's week. The week's picker lists the teams you
lead, or every team if your role looks after them all. A seat that may open no
team's week sees that this is not theirs. A lead asking about a team they are
not on gets **not found**.
